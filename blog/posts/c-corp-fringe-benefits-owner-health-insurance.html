C Corporation Fringe Benefits: Review Eligibility, Payments and Reporting

June 30, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A C corporation’s payment is not automatically deductible to the company and tax-free to its owner. Review each benefit separately: the recipient’s status, the plan, the expense, the employee exclusion, payroll reporting and the employer deduction. Adding unrelated limits together does not establish an eligible package.

Start with plan and recipient facts

Publication 15-B (2026) explains benefit-specific rules and exceptions. Ownership can matter through highly compensated or key-employee rules and nondiscrimination requirements. Being a C-corporation owner-employee does not remove every restriction.

Health coverage and reimbursement arrangements must fit together

The IRS’s medical insurance guidance explains that an employer offering a QSEHRA cannot also offer a group health plan to its employees. Do not add group-plan premiums and a QSEHRA limit as though they automatically form one permitted package. The 2026 annual QSEHRA maxima are $6,450 self-only and $13,100 family, with other requirements and potential proration.

Identify the actual arrangement and its terms before promising reimbursement. Keep proof of coverage, eligible expenses and amounts paid by insurance or another source. An expense already reimbursed elsewhere is not another unreimbursed claim. Different HRA structures have different conditions; they are not interchangeable labels for paying personal bills.

Educational assistance has an owner restriction

Section 127 provides a $5,250 exclusion for qualifying educational assistance in 2026, but also requires a qualifying program and limits owner-concentrated benefits. No more than 5% of the program’s annual amounts may benefit the specified class of more-than-5% owners and their spouses or dependents. An owner-only employee population cannot simply award itself $5,250 under that exclusion. Review employee eligibility, the ownership calculation and the whole program.

An employee exclusion and employer deduction are separate

Publication 15-B explains that qualified transportation benefits can be excluded from employee wages within applicable rules while the employer deduction is generally disallowed. A parking payment below an exclusion limit is not proof of a corporate deduction.

For life insurance, distinguish the face amount of coverage, the premium and any taxable cost under the applicable rules. $50,000 of coverage is not $50,000 of cash compensation. Review policy and employee classifications rather than adding coverage face amounts to paid benefits.

Retirement contributions are not permanently tax-free spending money

The IRS 401(k) contribution limits page gives a 2026 employee elective-deferral limit of $24,500 and an annual-additions limit of $72,000 before catch-up contributions, also subject to compensation and other rules. These are different limits, not amounts automatically available on top of each other. Plan terms, other contributions and applicable tests matter.

Publication 575 explains taxation of retirement distributions. Pretax employer funding is generally tax-deferred, not a guarantee of permanently tax-free withdrawals. Roth and after-tax treatment have separate rules. Keep retirement funding apart from currently spendable employee benefits.

Original fictional review: reconcile payments without approving exclusions

The medical claim is pending and no reimbursement has been paid. Eligibility, payroll and employer-deduction conclusions remain unresolved for every row.

This arithmetic establishes neither $50,250 of deductible expenses nor $50,250 of employee exclusions. It supplies no tax rate, wage gross-up or actual tax saving. A report should keep paid amounts, coverage limits, pending claims and approved treatment in separate fields.

Prepare a plan-by-plan decision record

Download a blank benefit review record (CSV). The employee exclusion, employer deduction and payroll reporting cells are separate and blank. A company payment alone does not fill them.

  • Identify the benefit, employee/owner status and applicable tax or plan year.
  • Locate the plan document, eligibility terms and any required employee-wide testing.
  • Match invoices and payment records; identify reimbursements and coordination with other coverage.
  • Document the exclusion or taxable amount, reporting and employer deduction separately.
  • Record open questions and the reviewer’s conclusion before treating a package as approved.

For a wider entity comparison, use company tax and owner cash. The right entity choice does not follow from a headline total of benefits.
