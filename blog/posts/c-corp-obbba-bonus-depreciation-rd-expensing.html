C Corporation Bonus Depreciation and R&D Expensing: Check Eligibility and Tax Use

July 7, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

The 2025 law changed depreciation and domestic research deductions, but a C corporation still needs eligible costs, the right dates and a usable deduction. A purchase price multiplied by 21% is not automatically a current refund or tax saving. Review the cost classification before comparing tax outcomes.

Bonus depreciation requires acquisition and service facts

The IRS’s Notice 2026-11 announcement explains the restored 100% additional first-year deduction for eligible property acquired after January 19, 2025. Publication 946 explains the acquisition and placed-in-service tests, qualifying property and exclusions. A delivery, deposit or invoice alone does not establish that an asset is ready and available for its assigned business use.

Retain binding contracts, acquisition dates, service evidence, adjusted basis, business use and any election. Used-property and related-party restrictions can matter. Do not treat every vehicle, building improvement, software bill or commercial building as one bonus-eligible class. A lease payment and the purchase of depreciable property also need different analyses.

Domestic research expensing and the research credit differ

Section 174A allows deductions for qualifying domestic research or experimental expenditures paid or incurred in tax years beginning after December 31, 2024, with an elective capitalization/amortization method for specified costs. Software development is addressed expressly, while acquisition of depreciable research property has separate treatment. A project’s label does not classify every invoice.

The section 41 research credit is a separate test, not a synonym for section 174A expensing. Review credit coordination and the actual activities and costs before claiming both. Vendor location alone does not establish where research was performed.

Section 174 continues to require capitalization and 15-year midpoint amortization for foreign research expenditures. Separate domestic and foreign work using supported records rather than putting all development spending into one current deduction.

Review prior-year research elections and filing history

The transition rules distinguish eligible small-business retroactive treatment from recovery of remaining unamortized domestic costs over one or two years beginning with the first tax year after 2024. They are different elections, not an automatic cash payment.

Revenue Procedure 2025-28 sets procedures and deadlines, including July 6, 2026 for specified amended-return/AAR small-business elections, subject to an earlier refund limitation. As of this November 22, 2026 review, that general date has passed. Check the actual election, filing dates, refund period and any applicable relief before proposing an amendment; do not assume a missed election remains freely available.

Qualified production property is a separate building rule

Section 168(n) provides an elective allowance for qualifying portions of nonresidential property integral to a qualified production activity. Generally, construction must begin after January 19, 2025 and before January 1, 2029, with service before January 1, 2031. Special acquisition rules have additional conditions.

Office, administrative, lodging, parking, sales, research, software-development and engineering areas are excluded. The taxpayer’s qualifying use, location, original use or special acquisition facts and election matter. A distribution center is not automatically eligible because of its industry label. Land is not a depreciable building cost. Review mixed-use portions and potential recapture rather than assigning a full facility purchase price to this allowance.

C corporation status does not remove every loss limit

Section 469 applies to closely held C corporations and personal service corporations as well as specified noncorporate taxpayers. Certain closely held C corporations can offset passive losses against net active income under a special rule; that is not unrestricted use against every type of income. An operating business’s own equipment is not automatically a passive investment either.

Establish the corporation’s classification and activity facts before comparing it with a pass-through. For a loss year, section 172 governs the NOL calculation and use. Carryforwards generally do not promise an immediate refund, and post-2017 losses generally face an 80% taxable-income limitation when used, subject to the statute’s details and exceptions.

Original fictional example: the same deduction, different current tax effect

Assume an invented ordinary C corporation in a 2026 tax year has $800,000 of equipment basis eligible for 100% bonus and $300,000 of domestic research costs eligible for current expensing. Eligibility and full deductibility are assumptions for this example, not findings about actual expenditures. There is no overlap between those costs.

Compare two alternative income scenarios, each with the same $1,100,000 assumed deduction. Use a flat 21% regular federal rate with no credits, prior losses, research-credit adjustment, other deduction limit, state tax or other tax. These figures compare income before and after the assumed deductions; they do not measure the incremental benefit of the 2025 law.

The lower-income case leaves a $600,000 arithmetic loss requiring a separate NOL determination and future-use review. It is not assigned an immediate cash value here. A deduction does not reimburse the $1,100,000 of costs or eliminate the need to finance spending. Any deferred benefit, recapture and shareholder effects need their own model.

Keep one supported cost-and-election file

Download a blank cost review record (CSV). Record the contract, acquisition and service dates; cost and basis; research location; potential overlap; election; limitations and reviewer conclusion. A blank eligibility decision is unresolved, not zero or approval.

Use the corporate and owner cash comparison before choosing an entity, and the business-purpose file guide for retained funds. Spending solely to obtain a deduction still reduces available cash.
