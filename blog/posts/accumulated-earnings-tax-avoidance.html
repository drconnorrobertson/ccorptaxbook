How to Review Accumulated Earnings Tax and Corporate Reserves

June 29, 2026 · Updated November 22, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

To address accumulated earnings tax, establish what the corporation needs its retained earnings for and review the applicable tax calculation. A bank balance, a board resolution or a working-capital formula alone does not establish that the tax is avoided. Keep the business-purpose evidence and the tax computation connected but distinct.

What the accumulated earnings tax applies to

Section 531 imposes an additional 20% tax on accumulated taxable income for corporations within section 532. Section 532 addresses earnings and profits accumulated to avoid shareholder income tax instead of being distributed, with specified exceptions. This is not simply a tax on every dollar a C corporation keeps.

Section 533 makes accumulation beyond reasonable business needs determinative of that purpose unless rebutted by the corporation under the specified evidentiary standard. It also gives mere holding or investment company status evidentiary significance. An operating company should not assume that an investment account or shareholder loan demonstrates its business need.

Cash, earnings and profits, and accumulated taxable income differ

A cash forecast answers when money is needed. Tax earnings and profits is a separate measure. Section 535 defines accumulated taxable income using taxable income with statutory adjustments, less the dividends-paid deduction and accumulated earnings credit. Neither book retained earnings nor cash minus a reserve automatically supplies that calculation.

What the $250,000 accumulated earnings credit means

Section 535(c) provides a minimum credit measured by $250,000 less accumulated earnings and profits at the preceding year-end, subject to its rules. Certain service corporations use $150,000 instead. Those fields include health, law, engineering, architecture, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts and consulting.

This is not a fresh $250,000 annual allowance or a rule that any bank balance below it is exempt. Mere holding or investment companies have a separate credit rule, and controlled-corporation limitations may apply. Have the reviewer establish the corporation’s classification, prior earnings and profits, current-year computation and relevant adjustments before assigning a credit. Cash above either figure is not automatically accumulated taxable income.

Support present needs and concrete future plans

Treasury Regulation 1.537-1 connects reserves to the corporation’s own bona fide business needs. Anticipated uses require specific, definite and feasible plans; indefinite intentions to expand someday are insufficient. Assess the facts at year-end and track whether plans proceed or change. A signed resolution should point to cost, timing and supporting facts rather than stand alone as a guarantee.

Regulation 1.537-2 lists fact-dependent grounds including expansion, equipment replacement, business debt and necessary working capital. It also identifies potential contrary indications, such as shareholder personal use, unrelated loans or investments, and unrealistic hazards. A category label alone does not settle the result. An investment in real estate needs its own connection to this corporation’s business; the asset name does not establish that connection.

Bardahl formula: a working-capital test, not automatic protection

The IRS’s Internal Revenue Manual, 4.10.13.2.5–2.5.1, discusses the operating-cycle approach associated with Bardahl. It describes a test that must fit the facts, with adjustments for seasonal or service businesses and fact-dependent treatment of payables. The manual guides examination; it does not turn a spreadsheet into a statutory safe harbor.

Before using an operating-cycle estimate, retain the inventory, receivable, payable and expense records behind it. Check whether the chosen period represents actual operations and whether the expenses or projects are also counted elsewhere. A generic number of days multiplied by rounded daily spending does not independently approve the whole reserve.

Original fictional example: remove financing and duplicate cash needs

Assume an invented company has $650,000 of available cash. Its draft packet includes the four records below. Assume $100,000 of equipment financing is committed and available, and the $180,000 operating forecast already includes the listed $40,000 debt principal. These assumptions are supplied for arithmetic only; no project is validated as a reasonable business need.

The $200,000 is not a determination of excessive earnings, accumulated taxable income, a required dividend or tax due. The $450,000 is not an approved accumulated earnings credit. If financing falls through or the forecast excludes that debt payment, the reconciliation changes. The tax review still needs earnings-and-profits records and all applicable statutory facts.

Build a reviewable purpose record

Download a blank corporate reserve purpose record (CSV). Enter the need, evidence and assumptions before recording a conclusion. Unknown amounts should remain unresolved rather than being entered as zero.

  • Attach dated quotes, contracts, forecasts or debt schedules and identify the year-end facts.
  • Record when the company expects to pay, any financing and overlap with other budgets.
  • Explain the business connection and track changes, cancellations or delays.
  • Keep the reviewer’s tax conclusion separate from the modeled cash requirement.

The companion business-purpose file guide explains how to organize the supporting records. For owner transactions, continue with salary, dividends, loans and reimbursements after confirming the relevant facts.
