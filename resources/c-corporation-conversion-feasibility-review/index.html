Before Electing C Corporation Tax Status: What a Feasibility Review Should Include

A profitable business owner may hear that C corporation taxation reduces the current tax bill. Before paying for an election or restructuring, commission a feasibility review that follows cash from the business to its owners and through an eventual exit. This is particularly important when the owner also holds real estate, needs regular distributions or expects to sell the business.

Describe the actual change being proposed

Ask the adviser to distinguish a federal tax classification election from a state-law conversion, a transfer of assets and the formation of a new company. These are different actions. An LLC may have federal corporate tax treatment without becoming a state-law corporation. The IRS explanation of LLC classification and Form 8832 guidance are useful starting points, but neither replaces analysis of an existing business.

The written scope should name the current entity, present tax classification, owners and proposed effective date. If the recommendation assumes a different legal entity will own contracts, employees or property, identify who will address those legal and operational changes. Do not approve an implementation described only as switching a box.

Give the adviser a usable owner cash forecast

Prepare a forecast separating money the business can retain from money owners expect to take home. Include payroll, distributions, debt payments, working capital, planned equipment, acquisitions and owner living costs. Use a normal year and a slower year so the recommendation does not depend on every dollar staying inside the company indefinitely.

A comparison should show the business tax and the consequences of the proposed payments to owners. Ask which assumptions are driving the result and whether the model includes applicable state taxes. A business-level rate comparison by itself does not answer how much cash the owner keeps after funds leave the company. IRS Publication 542 explains corporate tax and distribution concepts relevant to that distinction.

Keep the building separate in the analysis

If you own the operating property personally or through a separate entity, tell the adviser before any restructuring documents are prepared. Ask whether the proposal leaves ownership and leasing arrangements unchanged or contemplates moving the building. Include property debt, tax basis, depreciation, expected holding period and lender restrictions in the diligence packet.

A real estate transfer is not an incidental administrative step. It can introduce issues requiring separate tax and legal analysis. Ask for the reason the building belongs in the proposed structure, the alternative of leaving it where it is, and a written review of any transfer being recommended. A recommendation about operating-business taxation should not silently become a recommendation to transfer appreciated property.

Request entry, operating and exit scenarios

  • Entry: identify assets, liabilities, ownership changes, potential recognition issues and professional implementation costs.
  • Operations: compare retained profits with regular shareholder cash needs, payroll and benefits assumptions.
  • Exit: model a stock sale and an asset sale where relevant, instead of assuming a future buyer will accept the preferred structure.
  • Reversal: explain consequences and restrictions if owners later want a different classification.
  • State treatment: identify the states actually considered and any work excluded.

For example, a fictional owner planning years of reinvestment has different decision inputs from an owner who expects to distribute most profits and sell soon. The useful deliverable explains how the recommendation changes when those facts change. It should not present a single savings number without its assumptions.

Separate the recommendation from implementation

Request a written conclusion, material uncertainties, alternatives considered and the facts that would trigger another review. A feasibility engagement can reasonably conclude that no conversion is appropriate. That is still useful work if it prevents an expensive structure that does not fit the owner's plans.

If proceeding, establish who handles the tax election, legal documents, opening balances, payroll adjustments, state filings and confirmation records. Agree on completion evidence rather than treating a signed engagement letter as proof that the election became effective. Have the adviser identify any deadline-sensitive action before authorizing the change.

Prepare for a focused discussion

Discuss a business-owner entity review with a short description of the current entity, annual owner cash needs, property ownership and expected exit. Share confidential returns through the agreed secure process. Ask for the proposed review scope before assuming an election is the right answer.
