Corporate Owner Payments: Salary, Dividend, Loan, or Reimbursement?

Before a corporation pays an owner, determine what the payment represents. The accounting label should follow the transaction facts and tax rules rather than being selected after the cash moves.

Describe the underlying transaction

Identify whether the payment compensates services, distributes earnings, repays a documented obligation, reimburses business spending, or advances money. Collect the agreement, expense support, payroll record, or corporate approval relevant to that category.

Review compensation and dividends

Salary and dividends have different reporting and tax consequences. Corporations generally cannot deduct dividends. Compensation for services requires its own support and reporting. A payment should not be moved between categories solely to achieve a preferred result.

Review loans as real obligations

Document the lending arrangement and actual repayment conduct. Below-market shareholder loans can create tax consequences. Calling an advance a loan does not by itself establish loan treatment; the substance and applicable rules control.

Reconcile the ledger and filings

Match owner payment records to payroll, information reporting, financial statements, and tax returns. Resolve inconsistent treatment before filing. A clear owner transaction ledger reduces the chance of one payment being counted or characterized differently in multiple places.

Action checklist

  • Identify the payment purpose
  • Collect supporting records before payment
  • Review payroll and dividend consequences
  • Evaluate loan substance and terms
  • Reconcile the ledger with reporting

Can a shareholder withdraw corporate cash without classification?

Cash paid to an owner needs appropriate accounting and tax treatment. The result depends on the transaction facts.
