Corporate Cash Reserves: Document the Business Purpose

A corporation cash reserve should connect to the actual needs of the business. Document expansion, working capital, equipment, and other bona fide plans with budgets and decision records rather than treating every retained dollar as self-explanatory.

State the intended use

Identify the business need, expected cost, timing, and responsible person. Attach quotes, budgets, contracts, or other support where available. An indefinite statement that cash might be invested later is less informative than a defined operational plan.

Review the tax issues

The IRS explains that earnings retained beyond reasonable business needs may raise accumulated earnings tax concerns. Other rules can also matter depending on income and ownership. The appropriate review considers the corporation actual circumstances, not only its cash balance.

Separate shareholder use

Keep personal spending, owner compensation, dividends, and loans distinct from corporate projects. A business reserve is not automatically a tax-free source of personal cash. Have the advisor review transactions with owners based on their substance and documentation.

Refresh the reserve plan

Compare the original budget with actual spending and changes in the business. If a project is abandoned, update the record and reassess the intended use of cash. Retain the decision history so the annual return review can follow what changed.

Action checklist

  • Identify specific business needs
  • Attach budgets and timing
  • Review accumulated earnings concerns
  • Separate owner and corporate use
  • Update canceled or delayed projects

Is keeping cash in a corporation automatically improper?

No. The analysis considers bona fide business needs and other applicable rules. Document the reasons and obtain a facts-based review.
